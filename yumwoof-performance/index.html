Executive Summary

A retention-driven business with an inefficient acquisition engine

Yumwoof generated roughly $1.07M in revenue across 6,003 orders in the trailing four weeks, but the growth story and the acquisition story are two different things. Revenue is heavily carried by returning customers and direct traffic, while paid social acquisition is burning significant traffic volume for very little return.

The headline issue: new customer orders made up only about 12% of total order volume (720 of 6,003) over the period. This is a subscription-and-repeat business right now, not an acquisition machine, which raises the stakes on fixing paid social efficiency.

Key Takeaways

Three things to act on

1
Facebook Ads conversion rate is critically low

0.87% conversion against 42% of total site traffic (48,957 visits). This is by far the largest efficiency gap in the account and the highest-leverage fix available on the paid side. Traffic volume is not the problem, traffic quality is.

2
New organic content isn't gaining traction, likely tied to indexation bloat

Across the 57 blog and collection URLs tied to the current content strategy, only 3 pages generated any attributed revenue at all, and total attributed revenue across all of them was just $701. This lines up directly with the indexation findings from the AI Search audit: with 11,000+ pages competing for Google's confidence, new content has a hard time establishing authority and getting indexed with any real ranking strength. The content and indexation problems are the same root cause showing up in two reports.

3
AI Search visibility is close to zero

AI Search drove $1.23 in tracked revenue across the entire reporting window, effectively negligible. This matches the AI Search audit findings: Yumwoof lacks the off-page, third-party validated evidence AI engines need to cite or recommend the brand. Closing this gap means increasing off-page visibility, comparison articles, guest posts, listicles, and organic community presence, rather than anything on-site.

Content & Collection Page Attribution

SEO/content cluster is not yet converting

Across the 57 blog and collection URLs reviewed for the current content strategy, total attributed revenue was $701.34 from just 3 pages, against $1.07M in total tracked site revenue for the same window.

Several pages are pulling meaningful traffic without converting anyone: coconut-oil-dogs-teeth (402 visits), why-is-my-dog-throwing-up-undigested-food-hours-after-eating (223 visits), is-chicken-bad-for-dogs (98 visits), and can-i-feed-my-dog-rice-and-chicken-everyday (129 visits) combine for over 850 visits and zero attributed revenue. Roughly a third of the 57 URLs tracked had no recorded visits at all in this window.

Opportunity: the traffic exists on several of these pages already. The gap is conversion path, not demand. Stronger internal linking from high-traffic blog posts into the product/collection pages, plus clearer CTAs, is the highest-leverage fix before investing in more net-new content volume.

Data Notes

What this report could not measure

Ad spend, ROAS, CAC, impressions, and clicks all returned zero across every paid channel in ThoughtMetric for this project, meaning the Meta and Google ad account connections aren't currently feeding cost data into the platform. All paid channel figures above are traffic and conversion only, not efficiency against spend. Split/multi-touch attribution is happening under the hood (order counts by channel are fractional, e.g. 461.2 orders to Google Ads), but ThoughtMetric's available dimensions don't expose an order-level or touchpoint-count breakdown to quantify how many individual orders were multi-touch.

Recommendations

Priority actions

1

Reconnect or audit ad platform cost feeds

Without spend data, no paid channel decision can be made on real ROAS or CAC. This is the single highest-priority fix before touching budget allocation.

2

Reassess Facebook Ads targeting and creative

0.87% conversion against 42% of site traffic is not a small optimization problem. This warrants a full audience and creative review, or a temporary budget shift toward Google and email while it's fixed.

3

Clean up UTM tagging on Google Ads

The untagged Google Ads segment converts 3.5x better than the tagged segment. Fix attribution tagging so budget decisions reflect true campaign performance rather than a blended, misleading average.

4

Build internal links from high-traffic, zero-conversion blog content

Pages like coconut-oil-dogs-teeth and why-is-my-dog-throwing-up already have real traffic. Route it into product pages with contextual CTAs before producing more net-new content.

5

Protect against single-SKU concentration

Perfect Superfood is 79% of revenue. Cross-sell Perfect Kibble and the supplement line (Immunity, Probiotics, Digestion) more aggressively in post-purchase and email flows to diversify revenue.

6

Double down on new-customer acquisition offers

WELCOME10 drove only 52 orders in four weeks against 88% of the business being repeat. Test a stronger new-customer hook, especially paired with the trial SKUs that are already showing traction.
